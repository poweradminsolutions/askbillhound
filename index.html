The part they count on

Your bill didn't get bigger because you bought more. It got bigger because you stayed.

Cable, internet, phone, home security. They all run the same play: hook you with a promo rate, wait twelve months, then quietly ratchet it up. They have a name for it inside the building. It's called the loyalty tax, and it's priced on one bet: that you're too busy to sit on hold for 45 minutes and fight.

Play #1

The expired promo

The rate you signed up for was never the real rate. The real rate shows up in month 13, and it arrives without a phone call.

Play #2

The rented box

That $15/month "equipment fee" is a $60 modem you've now paid for six times. They'll keep charging until someone says stop.

Play #3

The invented fee

"Broadcast TV fee." "Regional sports fee." "Network enhancement." Line items built to sound official so you don't question them.

Straight answers

The questions you should be asking.

Is this legit?

Fair question. Bill negotiation is a real industry, and the honest version of it works exactly one way: no money up front, paid only from verified savings. That's how I run. You'll see every number before anything happens, and you approve the fight before I start it.

How can you negotiate for me?

You sign a one-page authorization that lets me deal with your provider on your behalf. Same thing a lawyer or a family member would use. You'll e-sign it from your phone in about a minute, and it only covers the bill you sent me.

Do I have to get on the phone?

No. That's the point of me. I handle the hold music, the retention department, the "let me transfer you." You get a text when it's done.

Will my service change?

Not unless you say yes first. My first move is always the same service at a lower price. If a better deal means a change, I bring it to you and you decide. Nothing moves without your go-ahead.

Where are your reviews?

I'm new, and I won't pretend otherwise. The first pack of hunts is underway right now, and real numbers from real bills get posted here as they land. No stock photos, no made-up names. If a company shows you a wall of five-star reviews the week they launched, ask who wrote them.
